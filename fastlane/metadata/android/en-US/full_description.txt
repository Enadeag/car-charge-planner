Car charge planner tells you when to charge your electric car at home, so that as much of your own solar energy as possible goes into the car and as little as possible is fed back to the grid. It suits a fixed-price energy contract: it does not look at hourly energy prices.

It is advisory only. From a solar forecast for your home it recommends a charge window and a charge power, and you set the charge current in your charger's app yourself. It reads nothing from the car: you enter the battery level the car shows by hand.

It plans from a description of your set-up, entered once in set-up and changed in Settings later: your solar panels (the number of panels, their direction and tilt, and the year they were placed or their power per panel), your location, the power your car charges with at home and your car's battery. Where you do not know a value, the app uses a typical value: replace it with yours when you know it, or the plan will not fit your home.

The dashboard has a tab for each day of the plan, with its charge window, the current to set, a chart of the expected solar power and the car's battery. If the battery would end a day below your minimum charge level, the grid advice says how much to charge from the grid and when. Enter a trip with its departure, return and distance, and the plan makes sure the battery holds enough at the departure. Once you turn on notifications, the app gives you a notice before each charge window starts, also while it is closed: it then asks the forecast provider about once an hour, and with Wi-Fi only switched on, only on Wi-Fi or another network without a data limit.

What leaves the phone: the app has no account, no tracking, no telemetry and no update check. It contacts only the forecast provider you chose (and a fallback, if you set one), and only to ask for a forecast. Open-Meteo, the default, is sent your location (latitude and longitude), your time zone and the tilt and direction of your panels; Forecast.Solar is sent your location, the tilt and direction of your panels and their size in kWp. With an API key, the key goes too. With Open-Meteo as your provider, the place you type in the place search goes to Open-Meteo's place search, with the app's language; with Forecast.Solar you type the coordinates instead. Like any website, the provider also sees your internet address. Your battery level, away periods, trips and the plan are never sent to a provider or anyone else; your settings, battery level, away periods and trips go only to your next phone, in Android's own transfer between two phones. API keys are kept encrypted, with a key in the Android Keystore.

The providers' free plans are for personal use; Open-Meteo's is for non-commercial use only.

The app works without the desktop app and without a server. It needs Android 10 or newer, does not use Google Play Services, Firebase or other proprietary Google libraries, and is in English and Dutch.
